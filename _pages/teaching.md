## Teaching Approach

My teaching begins with a simple premise: students already know something. While tutoring a graduate Program Evaluation course, a student asked whether a frequency table was the same as a contingency table. The barrier was not statistics. Different disciplines were using different terms for related ideas. That experience taught me to make disciplinary translation part of teaching rather than assume that unfamiliar terminology reflects a lack of knowledge.

In quantitative courses, students often encounter methods before understanding the problems those methods address. I reverse that order. Students first examine why sampling is necessary, where uncertainty enters a conclusion, what causal inference requires, and which assumptions support an analysis. Methods then become solutions to defined problems rather than formulas to memorize.

## Extension and Program Evaluation

### Growing Urban Agriculture Resilience and Development (GUARD)

I developed 13 ADA-compliant Canvas modules with embedded quizzes, instructor transcripts, and train-the-trainer materials for Florida Cooperative Extension educators across all 67 counties. The project reinforced that accessible materials benefit all participants, not only those requesting accommodations.

I now lead the program evaluation design, including a paired pre-post Theory of Planned Behavior instrument, pilot coordination, and quantitative analysis. The pilot includes 10 selected farmer reviewers and approximately 40 additional farmers, with a planned expansion to approximately 100 participants across additional Florida counties.

### Healthy Harvest

I am developing the implementation timeline and evaluation plan for county-level delivery. This work includes reviewing earlier paired pre-post data, assessing local-food and health outcome measures, and coordinating evaluation responsibilities with Extension agents, faculty collaborators, and undergraduate research support. The pilot includes approximately 40 participants in three Florida counties.
